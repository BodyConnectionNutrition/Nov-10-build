<section class="claim-series-intro">
  <div class="claim-series-thesis"><p class="eyebrow">The recurring question</p><h2>It sounded like information. But what did it actually tell you?</h2><p>A claim does not have to be demonstrably false to mislead. Sometimes its persuasive power comes from sounding more specific, scientific, complete, or medically authoritative than it actually is.</p></div>
  <div class="claim-series-context"><p class="eyebrow">Where this belongs</p><h2>See the system before blaming the consumer.</h2><p>This series is part of <strong>The Food Environment Is F’ing You</strong>. It shows how wellness advertising recruits scientific language, authority, abundance, purity, and social proof—then leaves the consumer to supply the promised conclusion.</p><p>Making that influence visible is part of recovering agency. The task is not perfect vigilance. It is refusing to give an advertisement credit for information it did not provide.</p></div>
</section>
