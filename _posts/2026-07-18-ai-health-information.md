AI systems generate plausible language from patterns in data. They can organize information quickly, but they can also combine accurate facts with false conclusions, omit important qualifications, or present unsettled claims as established science.

Health information is especially vulnerable because context matters. A statement may be true for one population, dose, diagnosis, or outcome and misleading when generalized.

## Why AI health answers can be misleading

A confident answer can hide uncertainty, missing context, weak evidence, or a leap from a limited study to a universal recommendation. Fluency is not the same as understanding, and confidence is not evidence.

## A better way to use AI for health questions

Treat an AI answer as a starting point for questions, not as clinical authority. Ask what evidence supports the claim, whether the sources are peer reviewed, how many studies agree, whether the research applies to the population in question, and what important exceptions were left out.

Useful questions include:

- What kind of evidence supports this claim?
- Is the conclusion stronger than the evidence allows?
- Does the research apply to the person, diagnosis, dose, and outcome being discussed?
- What would count as evidence against the claim?
- What important uncertainty or alternative explanation is missing?

The goal is not merely to distrust technology. It is to remain aware that the easiest answer to accept is often the one that confirms what we already hoped was true.
