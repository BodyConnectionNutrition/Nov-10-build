*What happens to the peel after the juice is gone*

The image used to sell orange juice is the inside of the fruit: bright segments, dripping juice and vitamin C. Industrial juice production must also account for everything surrounding that image—the peel, membranes, pulp and seeds left after extraction.

## What the extract is being sold to do

THE NAMED CLAIMS. NOW Foods says citrus bioflavonoids help maintain cellular health by neutralizing free radicals and support immune and vascular function [28]. Ingredient supplier IFF markets a citrus bioflavonoid complex for cardiovascular and metabolic health, including blood sugar, triglycerides, blood pressure and weight-management positioning [29].

## How peel becomes an ingredient

These citrus side streams are not chemically empty. Reviews describe them as sources of pectin, dietary fiber, essential oils, flavonoids, carotenoids and other compounds with potential food, cosmetic and pharmaceutical applications [12,13]. Pectin recovery is a mature example. Citrus peel can be processed into a gelling and stabilizing ingredient used throughout the food industry. Citrus oils contribute flavor and fragrance. Dried fibers can alter texture, water retention and fiber content.

## Where citrus recovery is unquestionably useful

NOW Foods is correct that citrus bioflavonoids are polyphenolic compounds found in citrus, while IFF is correct that citrus-processing streams can be standardized into traceable ingredients [28,29]. Citrus peel is also a well-established source of pectin, fiber and oils with measurable food functions [12,13]. These facts support ingredient identity and technological use, not every immune or metabolic implication.

## What the evidence says about concentrated extracts

THE EVIDENCE SPLIT. Citrus peel is an established source of pectin, fiber, oils and flavonoids [12,13]. That proves resource and technological value. It does not make a concentrated extract nutritionally equivalent to eating citrus fruit, nor does it establish every metabolic outcome listed in ingredient marketing.

## Borrowing credibility from the whole fruit

Citrus also benefits from a powerful naturalness halo. Consumers recognize the fruit and may mentally transfer everything they know about eating oranges to a concentrated ingredient recovered from industrial peel. But an isolated or concentrated component is not nutritionally interchangeable with the whole fruit. The whole fruit supplies water, carbohydrate, fiber and a complex food matrix. The extract supplies whatever the manufacturer selected and standardized.

## What the extract has not established

The named pages do not establish that their exact formulations reduce infections, improve clinically meaningful immune outcomes, prevent vascular events, or produce durable improvements in blood pressure, glycemia, triglycerides or body weight. Those outcomes require product-specific human trials rather than transfer of evidence from whole citrus fruit or isolated laboratory mechanisms.

## The commercial power of the fruit halo

Citrus processors can derive saleable pectin, fiber, oils and extracts from peel and pulp that would otherwise require management [12,13]. The investigation did not obtain company input costs or margins. Its commercial inference is narrower: a familiar fruit supplies both raw material and a preexisting health reputation, allowing the extract to inherit consumer trust that the concentrated product has not independently earned.
