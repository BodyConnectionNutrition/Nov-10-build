*How hides, bones and connective tissue became a wellness ritual*

Collagen marketing rarely begins at the slaughterhouse. It begins with luminous skin, glossy hair, strong nails and an elegant scoop dissolving into morning coffee. The supply chain is kept safely outside the frame.

## What is actually in the tub

THE NAMED CLAIM. Vital Proteins markets bovine collagen peptides as supporting hair, skin, nails, bones and joints [24]. Its marine collagen page identifies wild-caught cod scales as the source. The supply chain is not hidden in the ingredient description, but the front-facing consumer story remains beauty and bodily restoration.

## The remains of the animal supply chain

Commercial animal collagen is commonly recovered from materials generated by meat and seafood processing: bovine hides and bones, porcine skin, poultry tissues, and fish skin, scales and bones. Peer-reviewed reviews describe these materials explicitly as abundant processing by-products and examine methods for extracting collagen and converting it into gelatin or hydrolyzed collagen peptides [8,9].

## Why the evidence initially sounds convincing

Vital Proteins accurately identifies its product as hydrolyzed bovine collagen peptides, and collagen can be recovered from animal tissues such as hides, bones and connective tissue [8,24]. The company is also justified in pointing to a clinical literature: pooled randomized trials have reported changes in skin hydration, elasticity and wrinkles [25]. The dispute concerns the reliability and generalizability of that evidence, not whether studies exist.

## The result that changes the story

THE MOST IMPORTANT NEW EVIDENCE. A 2025 meta-analysis pooled 23 randomized trials involving 1,474 participants and initially found improvements in hydration, elasticity and wrinkles. When the authors separated trials by funding, studies classified as not receiving pharmaceutical-company funding showed no benefit, while studies classified as receiving pharmaceutical-company funding did [25]. That pattern does not prove fraud. It does mean that an unqualified statement that collagen is ‘clinically proven’ would conceal the most decision-relevant result.

## The anatomical shortcut in the marketing

Marketing often takes advantage of a linguistic shortcut: your skin contains collagen; this powder contains collagen; therefore the powder must rebuild your skin. The middle of that argument contains digestion, absorption, tissue allocation, protein adequacy, vitamin C availability, aging, ultraviolet exposure, smoking, genetics and many other variables. The shared word ‘collagen’ makes a complex physiological claim feel self-evident.

## What the trials still cannot establish

Independent evidence has not established that the marketed product produces clinically meaningful improvements across the full advertised list—hair, skin, nails, bones and joints—or that benefits persist long term. Most importantly, the 2025 funding-stratified meta-analysis found no skin-aging benefit in the subgroup classified as not receiving pharmaceutical-company funding [25].

## How animal tissue acquired a beauty-market identity

The documented retail snapshot was $49.99 for the Vital Proteins product page reviewed [24], and the scientific literature documents animal-processing tissues as collagen sources [8,9]. Extraction, hydrolysis, testing, flavor control, packaging and distribution add real cost. Because raw-material, bulk-peptide and company-margin data were not obtained, the article does not calculate a markup; it documents how the retail product is positioned around beauty, aging and mobility rather than around the former use of its source tissues.
