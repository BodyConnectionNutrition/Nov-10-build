You paid for the twelve-week program. You bought the powders. You told everyone this time would be different. Four weeks in, you are miserable—but stopping feels like wasting everything you invested.

So you invest more.

## Past investment is not a future obligation

A sunk cost is time, money, effort, or emotion that cannot be recovered. Rationally, the next decision should be based on future costs and benefits. Humanly, quitting can make the earlier investment feel wasted, so we continue in order to justify it.

The investment may include identity and hope. “I am a keto person.” “I am finally disciplined.” “This program is my last chance.” Leaving then feels like losing a version of yourself.

Persistence is valuable. Many meaningful efforts are uncomfortable before they pay off. We also want our choices to form a coherent story. Abandoning a path can feel like admitting that our judgment, sacrifice, or public commitment was wrong.

The problem is not commitment. It is allowing yesterday’s cost to purchase tomorrow without another evaluation.

## How programs deepen the commitment

Programs front-load payment, equipment, meal replacements, community membership, tracking streaks, and identity language. Subscription systems make cancellation harder than enrollment. Progress photos, badges, and escalating tiers create a visible investment history.

When results stall, the solution offered is often deeper commitment: buy the advanced plan, add coaching, tighten the rules, purchase the next supplement. Failure is attributed to insufficient adherence, protecting the program from evaluation.

People remain in restrictive diets that increase preoccupation, shame, social isolation, or physical symptoms. They keep unused products because discarding them would acknowledge the money is already gone. They postpone seeking appropriate care because they need the current investment to work.

The deepest cost may be interpretive. A system that failed becomes evidence that the person failed. The more she sacrificed, the more painful it becomes to examine the premise.

## Decide again using what you know now

Imagine you had not already paid, posted, tracked, or identified with the plan.

Knowing what you know today, would you choose it now?

What are the likely future costs of continuing?

What information would make stopping an act of learning rather than failure?

You can honor what you hoped for without giving the investment control over the next decision. The money is gone whether you continue or not. Your future time is not.

Quitting is not always wisdom. Neither is persistence. Agency means allowing new evidence to change what happens next, even when the old decision was expensive.

## A question to carry with you

Where are you continuing mainly because you have already invested so much? What would become possible if stopping were treated as information?

## Evidence and intent

The research above establishes cognitive mechanisms. It does not prove that every package, interface, campaign, or company intentionally uses a formally named bias. Observable design can be described directly; claims about a company’s intent require separate evidence of intent.
