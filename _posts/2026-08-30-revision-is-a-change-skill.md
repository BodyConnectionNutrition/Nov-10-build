Most plans are designed as if good judgment occurs only at the beginning.

The goal is chosen, the rules are set, and future success means fidelity. Revising the plan is treated as lowering the bar, making excuses, or losing motivation.

But plans are predictions. They estimate what a person, body, and environment will permit. New information should change the prediction.

## Feedback is useful only if change is allowed

A plan produces information: hunger increased, cost was higher than expected, preparation took too long, pain worsened, social support helped, the cue worked, the schedule did not.

If the person is not allowed to revise, feedback becomes evidence of failure rather than material for learning.

Permission to revise protects experimentation. It also prevents persistence from being confused with wisdom. Staying on an inadequate, restrictive, or inaccessible plan is not automatically perseverance.

## Maintenance includes recovery

Long-term change is not uninterrupted repetition. Contexts shift. Illness, travel, caregiving, work, finances, seasons, and bodies change. A pattern that cannot be adapted may disappear when its original conditions do.

Maintenance therefore includes restarting, scaling, substituting, recovering, and sometimes ending a goal that no longer serves its purpose.

This is particularly important in food and body work. A plan that intensifies preoccupation, restriction, binge eating, shame, or distrust may require prompt revision, not stricter enforcement.

## Make revision explicit

Notice the difference between the intended effect and the actual effect.

Interpret discrepancy as feedback.

Contextualize what changed in the person or setting. Was the original plan based on an unrealistic assumption? Did a once-useful support become burdensome?

Locate responsibility. The individual can report experience and preferences. Professionals are responsible for responding to harms, uncertainty, and changing needs rather than defending the original prescription.

Respond by naming what will remain, what will change, and when the new version will be reviewed. Preserve the purpose without worshiping the method.

Revision is not the opposite of commitment. It is commitment to reality.
