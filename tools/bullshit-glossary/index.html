The Bullshit Glossary

A field guide to language designed to sound more informative, scientific, or evidentiary than it is. This is not a test of whether a consumer is savvy enough to decode advertising. The company making money from a claim is responsible for making that claim clear and supporting it.

The question: What is the company getting these words to do that the evidence itself cannot do?

The governing principle

The burden belongs upstream.

You should not need a degree in chemistry, physiology, statistics, nutrition, or advertising to discover what a company means by the words it chose. If a seller uses technical language, invokes science, names a mechanism, or implies evidence, the burden of making that claim intelligible and substantiating it belongs to the seller.

This glossary examines commercial language. It does not diagnose motives, and inclusion does not by itself establish that a product is ineffective or that a claim is legally deceptive. It asks what the wording communicates, what it leaves unstated, and what evidence would be needed to support the impression it creates.

Educational resource only. Examples should be read in the context of the specific advertisement or product language in which they appear.
