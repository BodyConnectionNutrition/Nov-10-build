A free 1–2 minute practice

Interrupt the Verdict

Sometimes an experience and the judgment about it arrive so quickly that they feel like one event. This practice helps you find the small silence between them.

You do not need to replace the verdict with a positive thought. You do not need to solve the situation or decide what to do. The practice is the interruption.

Observation is not verdict.

Observation: “I ate past comfort.”

Verdict: “I have no self-control.”

The observation may deserve attention. The verdict closes the inquiry before it begins.

1 · Notice

Bring one recent moment to mind.

Choose something small enough to hold for a minute: an eating moment, a body sensation, a decision, a thought, or something you did not do.

Try removing identity words. “I skipped lunch” is an observation. “I am irresponsible” is a verdict.

2 · Interpret

What verdict arrived?

A verdict may sound like a fact, a character judgment, a prediction, a rule, or someone else’s voice.

3 · Interrupt

Let the verdict remain unfinished.

“A verdict is arising. It is an interpretation—not the whole event and not my identity.”

10

Nothing else is required during these ten seconds.

4 · Leave the space open

What can remain undecided?

What happened

Meaning that arrived

Both can be present without becoming the same thing.

“I don’t know yet” preserves inquiry. Uncertainty is not avoidance when the available explanation is incomplete.

Carry the practice

The interruption is the practice.

You did not have to correct the thought, prove it wrong, or produce a better behavior. You noticed that an interpretation was becoming a verdict and declined to collapse into it.

Use these four lines when a verdict appears:

1. What happened?

2. What verdict arrived?

3. “A verdict is arising.”

4. What can remain unknown for now?

Agency begins before action. The pause is a small act of participation: it keeps inherited judgment from becoming the only available meaning.

This practice is educational, not medical or mental-health treatment. You may stop at any time.
