You have already done the first important piece: separating what your body communicated from what your mind decided that communication meant.

Use this reflection once, or return to it when the signal appears again.

## 1 · Name the body information

What did you physically notice? Describe the sensation before explaining it.

Examples: pressure, warmth, emptiness, tightness, fading concentration, food thoughts, heaviness, restlessness, comfort.

## 2 · Name the interpretation

What explanation, prediction, rule, or judgment arrived next?

The interpretation may be useful, incomplete, uncertain, compassionate, habitual, or absent. You do not have to decide immediately whether it is correct.

## 3 · Restore the conditions

What else was participating in the moment?

- Time since eating, sleep, illness, medication, pain, or energy needs
- Stress, emotion, attention, sensory load, or competing demands
- Food rules, previous restriction, expectations, or learned meanings
- Access, time, privacy, safety, relationships, and the available choices

## 4 · Notice what happened next

Did you respond directly, wait, question the signal, compare it with a rule, postpone it, judge it, or remain unsure?

This is an observation—not a grade.

## 5 · Carry one open question

Finish this sentence:

> The next time I notice this signal, I want to remain curious about…

<div class="callout"><strong>“I don’t know yet” is a complete answer.</strong><br>The purpose is not to force certainty. It is to make room between sensation, interpretation, and response.</div>
