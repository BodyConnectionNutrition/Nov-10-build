<section class="about-story">
  <div><p class="eyebrow">Why I created this</p></div>
  <div><p class="about-lead">Again and again, I met people who had been given instructions when what they needed was an explanation.</p><p>They knew the rules. They had heard what they “should” eat, how they “should” look, and what a disciplined person was supposed to do. When those instructions did not fit their bodies or survive the conditions of their lives, they concluded that <em>they</em> were the problem.</p><p>But food behavior does not emerge from character. It emerges from a body with a history, a nervous system, learned expectations, relationships, culture, resources, competing demands, and an environment that is constantly participating.</p><p>I created Body Connection Nutrition to make those forces visible. This is a place to notice what your body is communicating, examine the frameworks that taught you what those signals mean, and rebuild what no longer fits. The purpose is not to make you better at obeying. It is to help you understand enough to become the author of what comes next.</p></div>
</section>

<section style="margin-top:5rem"><p class="eyebrow">An unusual combination</p><h2>Nutrition is only one part of the explanation.</h2><p class="lead">My work sits at the intersection of four fields that are usually separated. Bringing them together changes both the questions I ask and the answers people are able to find.</p><div class="about-expertise">
  <article class="card"><span class="number">1</span><h3>Nutrition science</h3><p>My graduate education in nutrition and food science and more than fifteen years in clinical care ground the work in physiology, metabolism, evidence, and the realities of feeding a human body.</p></article>
  <article class="card"><span class="number">2</span><h3>Neurodevelopment</h3><p>Postgraduate training in neurodevelopment and pediatrics taught me to look at sensory experience, attention, regulation, development, communication, and the many ways bodies make information available.</p></article>
  <article class="card"><span class="number">3</span><h3>Education</h3><p>My experience as a teacher and professor—and my study of transformational learning—means I do not confuse receiving information with being able to use it. Learning changes when people can examine the frameworks organizing what they believe.</p></article>
  <article class="card"><span class="number">4</span><h3>Systems thinking</h3><p>Family, culture, healthcare, economics, commercial environments, time, access, and institutional rules all participate in what becomes possible. I refuse to erase those conditions and call the remainder “choice.”</p></article>
</div><p>This combination is unusual because nutrition care is often narrowed to biology and recommendations, while education focuses on knowledge, neurodevelopment focuses on individual processing, and systems thinking focuses on conditions. I use all four to study the same human experience.</p></section>

<section class="about-human quote-card"><p class="eyebrow">What matters to me</p><h2>I am not interested in performing an idealized wellness life.</h2><p>I am interested in helping people understand the life and body they actually have. That means making room for uncertainty, competing needs, unfinished work, ordinary limitations, and the fact that essential care will not always look impressive from the outside.</p><p>You do not have to earn curiosity or compassion by becoming easier, healthier, thinner, more productive, or more compliant first.</p></section>

<section><p class="eyebrow">Professional background</p><h2>Credibility without distance.</h2><p>I am a registered dietitian with a master’s degree in nutrition and food science, a background in family and consumer sciences, postgraduate training in neurodevelopment and pediatrics, and graduate study in transformational leadership. I have worked across the lifespan in patient care and have spent years teaching people how to make difficult science useful.</p><p>Body Connection Nutrition is educational. Individual nutrition care is available separately through my virtual practice.</p></section>
