A working record, not a verdict.

You can answer, skip, leave unresolved, or stop at any point. Your responses stay in this browser page unless you choose to export them. They are not automatically saved.

1 · Notice

Start with something familiar

Choose a thought or message that keeps showing up. It might concern hunger, food, weight, health, or what a “good” person is supposed to eat.

You might consider a food rule, a message about your body, or something you are not yet sure how to describe.

2 · Interrupt

Make room before deciding

A thought can be familiar without being a fact. You do not have to agree, argue, or act on it right now.

3 · Deconstruct

Trace the message

Beliefs are learned in contexts. A belief may contain useful information, too. Investigate its authority rather than automatically rejecting it.

4 · Locate responsibility

Look beyond the individual

Your choices occur within systems you did not design. Consider who sets expectations and who has the power to change conditions.

5 · Reclaim agency

Choose what remains yours

Deconstruction does not require a replacement belief or an action plan. Agency can include uncertainty, seeking support, or deciding not to decide.
