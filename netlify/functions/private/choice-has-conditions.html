Conditions & Responsibility Map

Map the bodily, practical, relational, cultural, commercial, and institutional conditions surrounding one food-related difficulty—then locate responsibility without confusing causation, obligation, and control.

This tool does not erase agency or manufacture blame. It asks what contributed, who can reasonably be expected to respond, and what is actually possible under present conditions.

Causing, being responsible for, and being able to change something are three different questions.

1 · Choose one difficulty

Start with something you wish were easier.

Choose one food-, body-, or care-related decision, pattern, or situation that has been explained mainly as your personal responsibility.

2 · Widen the question

What conditions are participating?

A recommendation can sound simple when the conditions required to carry it out remain invisible. “Meal prep,” “eat regularly,” or “keep nourishing food available” all depend on time, money, access, storage, transportation, energy, predictability, competing demands, and more.

That does not mean choices are meaningless. It means choices happen inside systems that make some actions easier, harder, more expensive, more effortful, or temporarily unavailable.

Three questions will remain separate

Causal contribution: What helped produce the situation?

Ethical responsibility: Who can reasonably be expected to respond?

Practical control: What can actually be influenced under present conditions?

A condition can contribute causally without being your fault. You can hold some responsibility without having full control. And recognizing that responsibility belongs elsewhere does not make you passive.

10 · Locate responsibility accurately

The same condition can answer three questions differently.

The conditions you selected already belong in the causal explanation. For up to six of them, consider where responsibility sits and what practical influence is available now.

These are not rankings of effort or character. Responsibility can be shared. Control can be limited. “Unclear” and “not currently changeable” are accurate answers when they fit.
