A guided learning experience

Choice Has Conditions

Explore the practical, relational, cultural, and institutional conditions surrounding a food-related choice—and see which conditions widen, narrow, or reshape what is realistically available to you.

This tool does not remove agency. It makes the conditions around agency visible.

Knowing what might help is not the same thing as having equal access to every possible response.

1 · Choose one recurring decision

Start with something you wish were easier.

Pick one food-related decision or routine where you often think, “I know what I should do, so why don’t I do it?”

What decision are you exploring?
What explanation do you usually give yourself?

2 · Widen the question

What conditions are participating?

A recommendation can sound simple when the conditions required to carry it out remain invisible. “Meal prep,” “eat regularly,” or “keep nourishing food available” all depend on time, money, access, storage, transportation, energy, predictability, competing demands, and more.

That does not mean choices are meaningless. It means choices happen inside systems that make some actions easier, harder, more expensive, more effortful, or temporarily unavailable.

You are not deciding whether each condition is an excuse. You are identifying what is actually part of the decision landscape.

10 · What kind of condition is it?

Not every barrier calls for the same response.

Choose up to six conditions from what you noticed above that matter most for this decision. Then classify each one.

The categories are not rankings. “Not currently changeable” is not failure. Correctly recognizing a real constraint is useful information.
