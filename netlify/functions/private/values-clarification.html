Values are directions, not tests.

Notice the difference between inherited rules and the qualities you want your choices to express.

A value can matter even when circumstances make it difficult to express. Two values can pull in different directions. That is information, not failure.

Before you begin

A rule says, “I should.” A value says, “This matters to me.” Rules often demand one specific performance. Values can be expressed in many different ways.

2 · Make the words yours

3 · Notice the inherited rules

What has been directing the choice?

A useful distinction: values make room for authorship. Inherited rules often arrive attached to approval, safety, morality, belonging, or worth.

4 · Locate the conflict

Put the competing information in the same view.

5 · Translate a value into possibilities

A value should expand your options.

Check for hidden compliance

If the answer is complicated, that does not invalidate the value. It may show where diet culture, health pressure, scarcity, identity, relationships, or past protection strategies have become tangled with it.

6 · Choose one values-aligned experiment

An experiment is an opportunity to learn.

It is not a promise, prescription, test of motivation, or new rule.

7 · Reflect and revise

Update your understanding—not your score.
