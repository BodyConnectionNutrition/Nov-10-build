A Body Connection influence-mapping experience

How was my body image created?

Trace the experiences and influences that shaped how you learned to see, evaluate, and think about your body, then create a visual map of what still carries the most weight today.

This is not a score of whether your body image is good or bad. You are not being asked to love your body, correct your thoughts, or decide whether the messages you received were true.

The work here is earlier than that: to make the origins of body image visible.

Two different questions will appear throughout the experience.

First: Did this happen, or was this part of my world? Second: How much influence does this part of my history seem to have on how I experience my body now?

An experience can matter historically without feeling very influential today. Something that arrived later can also carry enormous weight now.

Influence 1 · Early body world

Early life

What did your first environment communicate about bodies?

Before you had a fully formed opinion about your body, you were already surrounded by information about bodies. Some of it may have been directed at you. Some may have been learned by watching how adults talked about themselves and other people.

Select experiences you personally remember having, seeing, or feeling in childhood.

How much influence does this early body world have on how you experience your body today?

This rating is about current influence, not whether the experiences were important when they happened.

Influence 2 · Growing up in a visible body

School years · puberty · adolescence

What happened when your body became social?

As the social world expands, the body can become more visible. Puberty, friendship, school, dating, sports, changing rooms, photographs, teasing, praise, attraction, and belonging can all teach what a body seems to earn, cost, or communicate.

Select experiences from school years, puberty, or adolescence that affected how you saw yourself.

How much influence do peer, puberty, and adolescent experiences have on your body image today?

You can have a long history in this area without it being the strongest influence in your life now.

Influence 3 · Culture, identity & belonging

Across the lifespan

Which bodies were treated as normal, desirable, respectable, or successful?

Body standards are not universal. Race, ethnicity, gender, sexuality, religion, class, generation, immigration, region, community, and identity can all shape which bodies are valued and what appearance is believed to communicate.

Select the cultural or identity-based experiences that were part of your world.

How much influence do culture, identity, and belonging have on your body image today?

Influence 4 · Media, images & commercial culture

Across the lifespan

Which bodies were repeatedly shown, rewarded, changed, and sold?

Media does more than show bodies. It teaches through repetition, comparison, editing, commercial promises, cameras, clothing systems, and algorithms. It can narrow what looks normal—or widen it.

Select experiences that shaped how you learned to see your own body or other bodies.

How much influence do media, images, and commercial culture have on your body image today?

Influence 5 · Healthcare, fitness & authority

Childhood through adulthood

What did formal authority teach you your body meant?

Doctors, coaches, trainers, health education, fitness spaces, measurements, diagnoses, and weight-focused systems can shape body meaning. This overlaps with food and restriction, but the focus here is specifically on what these encounters taught you about your body.

Select experiences that influenced how healthy, capable, acceptable, risky, disciplined, or changeable your body seemed.

How much influence do healthcare, fitness, and formal authority have on your body image today?

Influence 6 · Living in a changing adult body

Adult life · current life

What has continuing to live in your body taught you?

Body image does not stop developing after adolescence. Relationships, reproduction, aging, illness, disability, weight change, sexuality, work, caregiving, function, loss, recovery, and changing priorities can all revise what the body means.

Select adult experiences that have shaped how you see or evaluate your body.

How much influence does adult lived experience have on your body image today?

HOW I EXPERIENCE AND EVALUATE MY BODY TODAY

The framework has a history.

Your map does not tell you whether the messages you received were true, helpful, harmful, or worth keeping. It shows something earlier and more fundamental: the way you experience your body did not develop in isolation.

Bodies are lived privately, but body image is learned in relationship with families, peers, development, communities, institutions, culture, media, authority, and changing life experience. None of these influences had to be intentional to become part of the framework through which you learned to see yourself.

You do not need to change anything on this map. For now, the work is simply to see that the framework has a history.
