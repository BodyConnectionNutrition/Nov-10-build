A guided learning experience

What is this doing for me?

Examine one recurring food- or body-related pattern and identify what it may provide, accomplish, protect, regulate, or make possible.

This tool is not asking whether the pattern is good or bad. A behavior can make sense and still have consequences you do not like.

Instead of asking only, “Why do I keep doing this?” try asking: what changes for me when this pattern happens?

1 · Choose one recurring pattern

Pick something that happens more than once.

The pattern can involve eating, delaying eating, grazing, checking, compensating, avoiding, planning, body checking, exercise, or another repeated food/body behavior.

What pattern are you exploring?
How do you usually explain this pattern?

2 · Function is different from approval

A pattern can be useful and costly at the same time.

Repeated behaviors often persist because something changes when they occur. They may create relief, stimulation, predictability, energy, connection, distance, comfort, structure, or a sense of control. Sometimes the function is obvious. Sometimes it is not.

Identifying function does not mean endorsing the behavior. It means asking what the behavior is doing inside the system in which it appears.

Two things can be true.

This pattern may help in the short term and create consequences later. The goal is to see both without forcing one truth to cancel the other.

3 · When does it appear?

Look for the conditions around the pattern.

You do not need a perfect trigger. We are looking for recurring circumstances that make the pattern more likely or more useful.

What conditions do you notice in your own words?

4 · What changes immediately?

What becomes different when the pattern happens?

Focus on the short-term effect, even if the effect is brief.

Complete this sentence if you can: “When I do this, it gives me...”

5 · Possible functions

What job might the pattern be doing?

Select any that seem plausible. You do not need to choose one “real” function.

Is there another function not listed?

6 · What happens later?

Now include the costs and consequences.

A short-term benefit and a later cost can coexist. Neither erases the other.

What later consequences matter to you?
